1 · Rating players

Elo expectation: who should win?

EA = 11 + 10(RB − RA) / 400
In English
chance A wins = 1 ÷ ( 1 + 10 ^ ( rating gap ÷ 400 ) )
rating gap = B's rating − A's rating  ·  chance B wins = 1 − chance A wins

MeaningOnly the gap matters. 400 points of gap = ten times more likely to win. Equal ratings = fifty fifty.

Real numbersMarcus 1685 vs Sofia 1333: chance Marcus wins = 0.88, so chance Sofia wins = 0.12.

1 · Rating players

Elo update: how ratings move

R′ = R + K(S − E),   K = 32
In English
new rating = old rating + 32 × ( result − expected chance )
result = 1 for a win, 0 for a loss

MeaningRatings move by how surprising the result was. Expected wins barely move; upsets move a lot. Winner's gain = loser's loss, points are conserved.

Real numbersSofia (0.12 expected) upsets Marcus: 1333 + 32 × (1 − 0.12) = +28 for her, −28 for him. If Marcus wins as expected, only ±3.8.

1 · Rating players

The race model: racks to matches

P(N, q) = N−1Σk=0 C(N−1+k, k)   qN (1−q)k
In English
match chance = add up, for every score the loser could end with: (ways that score can happen) × (chance of that rack sequence)
a match is a race: first to 5 racks, the final first to 7

MeaningLong races favor the favorite too much, so I solve this backwards: find the per rack chance whose match odds equal the Elo odds exactly.

Real numbersElo says 0.703. Solving backwards gives rack chance 0.586. Plugging 0.703 in directly would inflate the match odds to 0.905, unfair to the underdog.

2 · Counting the event

Total matches

|E(Kn)| = n(n−1)2
In English
total matches = players × (players − 1) ÷ 2
each player faces everyone else once; divide by two so each match is not counted from both sides

MeaningPlayers are dots, matches are lines between them: everyone playing everyone is the complete graph.

Real numbers8 players: 8 × 7 ÷ 2 = 28 matches. 30 players: 30 × 29 ÷ 2 = 435.

2 · Counting the event

Real event duration

racks ≈ N + N−12
In English
event time = rounds × waves × racks per match × minutes per rack
racks per match ≈ race length + half of (race length − 1)  ·  waves = matches per round ÷ tables, rounded up

MeaningThe winner plays exactly 5 racks, the loser averages about 2, so about 7 racks a match. Rounds run one after another; matches share the Union's six tables.

Real numbers8 players: about 6.5 hours, one long evening. 30 players: over 80 hours. Small fields fit a night; big fields are simulation territory.

3 · Scheduling

A schedule is a one-factorization

E(Kn) = M1 ∪ M2 ∪ … ∪ Mn−1
In English
all the matchups = round 1's pairs + round 2's pairs + … + the last round's pairs
every matchup appears in exactly one round, and nobody plays twice in the same round

MeaningSplitting all the matches into clean rounds like this is called a one-factorization. Scheduling a round robin IS this splitting problem.

Real numbers6 players: 15 matchups = 5 rounds × 3 matches.

3 · Scheduling · the one to own

The circle method, and why it works

i + j ≡ −2r  (mod n−1)
In English
seat i + seat j = −2 × round number (working mod players − 1)
two players meet in the one round whose number matches their seat sum

MeaningOne player sits fixed, the rest rotate around a ring, pairing across. Each pair of seats has one fixed sum, and the rounds sweep every possible sum exactly once, because 2 divides cleanly mod an odd number. One sum each, every sum hit once: every pair meets exactly once.

3 · Scheduling

Why odd fields need byes

χ′(Kn) = n−1  (n even),    n  (n odd)
In English
rounds needed = players − 1 if even, players if odd
with an odd count, every round must leave exactly one player resting

MeaningThat is the graph theory reason byes exist, it is forced, not a design choice. The app adds a phantom seat so each player rests exactly once.

Real numbers13 players: 13 rounds, one rotating bye per round.

4 · The knockout

Seeding the bracket

S(1) = [1],   S(2k) = [s,  2k+1−s] for each s ∈ S(k)
In English
pair every seed with its mirror: seed s plays seed (bracket size + 1 − s)
so every first round pair adds to the same number: best vs worst, second best vs second worst

MeaningThe top seeds land in different branches of the tree, and two players can only meet where their branches join. That is why seeds 1 and 2 can only ever meet in the final.

Real numbers8 seeds come out 1, 8, 4, 5, 2, 7, 3, 6: every opening pair sums to 9.

4 · The knockout

How many matches? Count losses

single: k−1,    double: 2(k−1) + {0, 1}
In English
single elim: matches = players − 1   ·   double elim: matches = 2 × (players − 1), plus 1 if the grand final resets
every match creates exactly one loss, and in double elim every non champion exits with exactly two

MeaningCount losses instead of matches and both formats fall out in one line. The reset happens when the losers champion wins the first grand final game.

Real numbers8 seeds: single is 7 matches, double is 14, or 15 with a reset.

5 · An honest limitation

Carry-over imbalance

Γ = Σj,k G[j][k]2
In English
imbalance = add up ( every handoff count ) squared  ·  fairest possible = players × (players − 1)
a handoff: you play someone right after they faced a bruising opponent, inheriting their fatigue

MeaningA 2018 theorem proves my schedule is the worst possible by this measure. I show it on purpose: the method with the one line proof costs carry-over balance, and stating the tradeoff honestly is part of the design.

Real numbers6 players: my schedule scores 60, the fairest possible is 30, exactly twice as bad. Balanced schedules exist when the player count is a power of two.
